# ppl oracle pin sheet — densities, Edgeworth/Cornish-Fisher, conjugates

Generated by the standalone reference prototype `oracles/ppl/MomentAlgebra.fsproj`,
which is deliberately **not** referenced by `Blade.fsproj`. Its algebra
duplicates what `src/ppl/compiler/PplElaborate.fs` will emit; that duplication
is what makes it an independent oracle (see `docs/plan-ppl-proper.md` §0, §8).

Every block below is the verbatim output of the command line printed above it.
Regenerate with the same command after any change to `oracles/ppl/Density.fs` and
re-diff before touching a corpus EXPECT.

## How to use these numbers

- **Values are 17 significant digits** (`%.17g`), which round-trips an IEEE
  double exactly. Input columns (`x=`, `p=`, prior hyperparameters, data) use
  the *shortest* round-tripping form instead, because that is what a corpus
  author types.
- **Compare with a tolerance, never bitwise.** The oracle's `lgamma` is a local
  Lanczos g=7/n=9 approximation and is *not* the bit-exact runtime/interp
  mirror the compiler needs (`docs/plan-ppl-proper.md` §8). Agreement between
  the two sides is expected at ~1e-12 relative, not at the last ulp. The
  lgamma-free families (gaussian, exponential, uniform, lognormal) have no such
  caveat.
- `-inf` means "outside the support". The convention is: a point strictly
  outside the support, or on a boundary where the density is undefined,
  reports `-inf` rather than raising. Invalid *parameters* raise instead.
- `lgamma` is pinned exactly at its zeros `x = 1` and `x = 2` (the usual
  Cephes/Boost practice), so `beta(1,1)` logpdf, the Poisson log-pmf at
  `k = 0`, and the identity `Gamma(1, r) == Exp(r)` all come out clean.
- The **Edgeworth density can go negative** — see the negativity probe at the
  end of the `dump-edgeworth` block. That is not a bug in the oracle; it is the
  documented failure mode of the expansion, and the compiler side pairs it with
  `dist_negativity` (plan §2).
- The **Cornish-Fisher quantile is an approximation** except on a Gaussian
  tower, where it is exact. On the `exponential(1)` tower at p = 0.05 it is off
  by +6.9e-2 (r=4) and +2.4e-2 (r=6); pin the oracle's value, not the exact
  quantile of the underlying law.
- AS241 is *not* exactly antisymmetric across p = 0.5 in floating point:
  `z(0.05) = -1.6448536269514726` but `z(0.95) = 1.6448536269514715`, because
  `1 - 0.95` is not `0.05` as a double and the two tails take the same rational
  branch with different arguments. Expected; do not "fix" it.

Notes for corpus authors:

- **Beta-Bernoulli and the μ-margin of Normal-InverseGamma get no tower.** Beta
  has no cumulant constructor in `Dist.fs`, and the μ-margin is a Student-t
  with 2·α_n = 9 degrees of freedom, whose cumulants past order 9 do not exist.
  Closed-form moments are published instead. The posterior *precision*
  τ = 1/σ² **is** Gamma(α_n, β_n), so that one does get a tower.
- `post_mean` values like `0.59999999999999998` (exactly 9/15) and
  `5.6999999999999993` (the sum of the data as doubles) are the honest
  double-precision results of the stated arithmetic — reproduce the arithmetic,
  do not round the pin.
- The hyperparameters that are exact small integers (`post_a=9`, `post_b=6`,
  `post_shape=16`, `post_rate=6`, `post_kappa=6`, `post_alpha=4.5`) are safe to
  pin as integer literals.
- Hand-checkable: Normal-Normal posterior variance is 1/(1/4 + 5/2) = 4/11 =
  `0.36363636363636365`; Gamma-Poisson is Gamma(2+14, 1+5) = Gamma(16, 6) with
  mean 8/3 and variance 4/9; Normal-InverseGamma has
  x̄ = 1.14, SS = 4.492, so α_n = 2 + 5/2 = 4.5 and
  β_n = 3 + 2.246 + 1·5·1.14²/12 = `5.7875000000000005`.
